# Best Tools to Turn Long Videos into Shorts in 2026

Turning a long video into Shorts is not just cutting a 60-second section.

A good Short needs a hook, enough context, clean captions, the right aspect ratio, and an ending that does not feel like it was chopped off by accident.

That is why different tools work for different creators. A podcast host needs one workflow. A movie recap channel needs another. A marketing team repurposing webinars needs another. A solo creator may care more about free exports than team collaboration.

This guide compares the most practical tools for turning long videos into YouTube Shorts, TikToks, and Instagram Reels.

## Before you choose a tool

Ask one question first:

> Do you need the tool to find the clip, or only to edit the clip?

If you already know the exact moment you want to post, use a manual editor like CapCut, VEED, Descript, or Kapwing.

If you want the tool to find moments for you, use OpusClip, Vizard, Klap, or NarratoAI.

If you record the source video inside the tool, Riverside may make sense.

## 2. OpusClip — best one-click long-video-to-Shorts tool

OpusClip is built around the exact job of turning long videos into short clips. Its homepage describes the product as turning a long video into Shorts and publishing to social platforms in one click. Its free plan includes 60 credits per month, up to 1080p rendered clips, auto reframe, AI captions, and a watermark.  
Source: [OpusClip pricing](https://www.opus.pro/pricing)

### What works well

OpusClip is a strong first test for:

- podcasts
- interviews
- educational YouTube videos
- creator talking-head videos
- business videos

It gives you a fast answer to the question: “Can AI find useful moments in this video?”

### Where it may fall short

The free plan is watermarked and limited. Also, the AI may choose moments that sound energetic but do not carry enough context for a good standalone Short.

## 3. Vizard — best for simple long-video repurposing

Vizard’s free plan includes 60 credits per month, 720p export, AI-generated clips, full editor access, and 3-day storage. Its Creator plan is positioned around no watermark, 4K export, scheduling, and permanent storage.  
Source: [Vizard pricing](https://vizard.ai/pricing)

### What works well

Vizard is practical for repurposing:

- webinars
- podcasts
- interviews
- business videos
- educational content

The workflow is easy to understand: upload, get AI clips, edit, export.

### Where it may fall short

If you need a Short that follows a narrative structure, test carefully. A general repurposing tool may not always pick the context you need.

## 4. Klap — best for podcasts and talking-head videos

Klap’s entry plan is listed at $23/month when billed yearly, with 10 video uploads monthly, videos up to 45 minutes, 100 clips monthly, and HD downloads.  
Source: [Klap pricing](https://klap.app/pricing)

### What works well

Klap is focused and simple. It is a good match for creators who want to repurpose:

- podcasts
- interviews
- talking-head YouTube videos
- educational clips

### Where it may fall short

The entry plan has limits on video uploads, video length, and clip volume. If you process long livestreams or a high volume of videos, check the higher plans.

## 5. Descript — best when the transcript matters

Descript is useful when turning long videos into Shorts is part of a larger editing process.

Its free plan includes 60 media minutes per month and 100 one-time AI credits. Its Hobbyist plan includes 10 media hours, 400 AI credits, 1080p watermark-free export, and access to AI tools. Its Creator plan includes 30 media hours, 800 AI credits, and 4K watermark-free export.  
Source: [Descript pricing](https://www.descript.com/pricing)

### What works well

Descript shines when you want to edit with text. You can clean up speech, remove filler words, edit the transcript, and shape the final clip with more control.

### Where it may fall short

It is not the fastest “generate 10 Shorts now” tool. It is better when quality and control matter more than speed.

## 6. CapCut — best free finishing editor

CapCut’s online editor is positioned as free and AI-powered, with HD export without watermark for YouTube, TikTok, and Reels. Its site also lists auto subtitles, background removal, templates, text-to-speech, and other social editing tools.  
Source: [CapCut homepage](https://www.capcut.com/)

### What works well

CapCut is excellent after you already know which clip you want.

Use it for:

- caption styling
- social templates
- manual cuts
- effects
- music
- quick mobile editing

### Where it may fall short

CapCut is not always the best tool for automatically finding the right moments in a long video. Pair it with a clip generator if you want automation.

## 7. VEED — best browser editor for quick Shorts

VEED’s free video editor page says free users get basic editing tools, 720p export, and one-time trials of some AI video editing tools. It also says free exports include a watermark, and upgrading unlocks watermark-free videos and more AI features.  
Source: [VEED video editor](https://www.veed.io/tools/video-editor)

### What works well

VEED is useful for quick browser-based editing:

- captions
- resizing
- cropping
- text
- audio cleanup
- simple social videos

### Where it may fall short

The watermark means the free plan is mainly for testing. For real publishing, expect to upgrade.

## 8. Kapwing — best for teams making many formats

Kapwing’s Pro plan supports no watermark, uploads up to 6GB, 4K exports, projects up to 2 hours, brand kit, custom fonts, collaboration, and up to 1,000 minutes of auto-subtitling per month. The free plan is limited to 1-minute exports with a watermark.  
Source: [Kapwing pricing](https://www.kapwing.com/pricing)

### What works well

Kapwing is a good fit for:

- small marketing teams
- agencies
- educators
- teams needing comments and shared projects
- content that needs many aspect ratios

### Where it may fall short

It is less attractive for solo creators who only need automatic clip selection and nothing else.

## 9. Riverside — best for recording first, then making clips

Riverside is a recording-first platform that also includes editing, repurposing, social clips, transcriptions, streaming, and publishing. The homepage says users can start for free with no credit card.  
Source: [Riverside homepage](https://riverside.com/)

### What works well

Riverside is a good choice when your long video starts as a remote recording:

- podcasts
- interviews
- panels
- webinars
- founder conversations

### Where it may fall short

If your videos already live on YouTube or in Google Drive and you only need a clipping tool, Riverside may be more than you need.

## Suggested workflow

2. Use an editor to polish captions, pacing, titles, and visuals.

- Podcast creator: OpusClip or Klap → CapCut
- Webinar marketer: Vizard → Kapwing

Many AI clipping tools support YouTube links or long-video uploads. Check each tool’s supported sources, copyright rules, and export limits before using third-party content.

### What is the best free tool to turn long videos into Shorts?

CapCut is strong for free manual editing. OpusClip and Vizard are easy to test for AI clipping, but free plans have limits. For publishing, check watermarks carefully.

### What is the best tool for movie recap Shorts?

Use a tool built around story structure. NarratoAI fits here if it can create clips with setup, context, and payoff.
